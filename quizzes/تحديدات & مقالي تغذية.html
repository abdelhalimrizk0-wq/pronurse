III. Essay Questions

10 detailed questions
Q1

List the core principles of a heart-healthy diet.

  • Fruits & Vegetables: Aim for 4–5 servings of each per day.
  • Whole Grains: Opt for whole grains over refined grains.
  • Lean Protein Sources: Fish, poultry, legumes, low-fat dairy.
  • Healthy Fats: Focus on monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats; limit saturated and trans fats.
  • Limit Sodium: Reduce to less than 2,300 mg/day, ideally 1,500 mg/day.
  • Limit Added Sugars: Minimize sugary beverages and desserts.
  • Moderate Alcohol: If consumed, do so in moderation.
Q2

Enumerate the benefits of the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH diet).

  • Blood Pressure Reduction: Clinically proven to lower both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
  • Cholesterol Improvement: Can help lower LDL cholesterol levels.
  • Weight Management: Promotes a balanced intake of nutrient-dense foods, aiding in weight control.
  • Reduced Risk of Other Diseases: Associated with a lower risk of heart disease, stroke, diabetes, and some cancers.
Q3

Signs of Good Nutrition

  • A Well-Developed Body
  • Ideal Weight for Height
  • Healthy Skin
  • Glossy Hair
  • Clear and Bright Eyes
  • Normal Appetite, Digestion, and Elimination
  • Mentally and Physically Alert
  • Ability to Resist Infectious Diseases
  • Increasing Life Expectancy
Q4

Function of Nutrients (Diet)

  • Provide Energy: Specially carbohydrates and fats, essential for metabolic processes.
  • Build Tissue: Proteins are primary building blocks (growth, repair, maintenance); minerals like calcium for bones.
  • Regulate Metabolic Processes: Vitamins and minerals act as coenzymes/cofactors in biochemical reactions.
  • Maintain Optimal Health: Support immune function, maintain fluid balance, and proper nerve/muscle function.
  • Preventing Chronic Diseases: Adequate nutrition prevents heart disease, diabetes, etc.
Q5

Characters of Desirable / Optimal Nutrition

  • The individual receives and effectively utilizes all essential nutrients from a varied and balanced diet in appropriate amounts.
  • Body tissues have sufficient nutrient reserves to support normal metabolic functions and maintain stores for periods of increased demand.
  • Characterized by robust health, high energy levels, and a strong immune system.
Q6

Factors affecting nutrition requirements

  • Age: Infants, children, elderly have different requirements.
  • Sex: Different needs between males and females.
  • Pregnancy & Lactation: Increased nutrient needs (↑).
  • Illness or Injury: Increased protein and energy needs (↑).
  • Physical Activity Level: Higher activity increases caloric and nutrient demands.
  • Environmental Conditions: Climate and surroundings affect nutritional requirements.
Q7

Key Principles of Therapeutic Nutrition (Diet)

  • Customization: Highly individualized based on diagnosis, age, weight, and lifestyle.
  • Disease Management: Manage or alleviate symptoms (e.g., low-sodium for hypertension).
  • Nutrient Correction: Correct deficiencies (e.g., iron-rich diet for anemia).
  • Support & Healing: Aid healing process, especially post-surgery or illness (e.g., high protein diet).
  • Prevention of Complications: A controlled-carbohydrate diet helps prevent dangerously high blood sugar in diabetic patients.
Q8

Key Objectives of Nutritional Therapy in Diabetes

  • Glycemic Control: Maintain blood glucose levels within a target range.
  • Weight Management: Achieve and maintain healthy body weight.
  • Prevention of Complications: Reduce risk of acute and chronic complications (cardiovascular disease, nephropathy, etc.).
  • Lipid Profile Improvement: Optimize blood lipid levels to reduce cardiovascular risk.
  • Individualization: Develop a personalized eating plan.
Q9

Meal Planning Strategies for DM (Diabetes Mellitus)

  • Regular Mealtimes: Encourage consistent mealtimes to prevent extreme fluctuations.
  • Balanced Meals: Each meal should include a balance of carbohydrates, proteins, and healthy fats.
  • Snacks: Healthy snacks can help prevent hypoglycemia (especially for individuals on insulin).
  • Portion Control: Emphasize the importance of portion control for all food groups.
Q10

Nursing Implications (instructions) for Osteoporosis

  • The importance of a balanced diet rich in calcium and vitamin D.
  • Reading food labels to identify sources of these nutrients.
  • The role of other nutrients and lifestyle factors in bone health.
  • The benefits of regular physical activity.
  • The risks associated with excessive sodium, caffeine, and alcohol intake.
